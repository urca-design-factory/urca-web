# CREATIVE-DIRECTION.md

## Urca Design Factory — Creative Direction

### 1. Brand Idea

**Urca Design Factory turns ideas into identities, products and digital experiences.**

Urca is an independent design and technology studio working across brand, AI-powered software, digital products, mobile applications and interactive experiences.

The word **Factory** represents a process of transformation:

**Idea → Strategy → Identity → Product → Build → Launch → Evolve**

Urca should not feel like a conventional full-service agency or a generic software company. It should feel like a highly capable creative production system where strategy, design and engineering coexist.

---

## 2. Positioning

### Primary positioning

**We design ideas.  
We build experiences.**

### Supporting statement

Brand identities, AI-powered products, software and interactive experiences — from first idea to launch and beyond.

### Core promise

**End-to-end professional execution without fragmentation between strategy, design and engineering.**

The website should communicate that Urca is capable of taking responsibility for an entire project rather than delivering isolated pieces.

---

## 3. Audience

Urca operates globally.

Primary audiences:

- Founders
- Startups
- Scale-ups
- Established companies launching new digital products
- Brands undergoing transformation
- Organizations exploring AI-powered products or automation
- Teams requiring a single partner across brand, product and development

The visual language should therefore feel international rather than geographically specific.

---

## 4. Brand Personality

Urca should feel:

- Intelligent
- Precise
- Contemporary
- Experimental
- Crafted
- Confident
- Curious
- Technically capable
- Design-led
- Calm rather than loud

It should never feel:

- Corporate
- Template-driven
- Trend-chasing
- Crypto/cyberpunk
- Generic SaaS
- Childishly playful
- Overly futuristic
- Visually noisy

---

## 5. Creative Concept

### Editorial × Computational × Crafted

The identity combines three visual worlds.

**Editorial**

Large typography, asymmetrical compositions, deliberate whitespace and strong hierarchy.

**Computational**

Generative ASCII, character-based graphics, systematic motion and subtle procedural behavior.

**Crafted**

Every element should feel intentional. Interactions, typography and visual details are treated as designed objects rather than UI defaults.

---

## 6. Experimental Level

**7 / 10**

The site should be experimental enough to be memorable but controlled enough to establish trust with global professional clients.

Rule:

> The first impression should be confidence.  
> The second impression should be curiosity.

Experimental elements should reward exploration rather than obstruct comprehension.

---

## 7. Visual Principles

### Typography first

Typography is the primary visual device.

Large headlines may occupy substantial portions of the viewport.

Avoid filling empty space simply because it exists.

Whitespace is an active part of the composition.

---

### Editorial composition

Prefer:

- Large typography
- Oversized media
- Asymmetrical grids
- Unexpected alignment
- Negative space
- Strong vertical rhythm
- Small technical metadata
- Minimal framing

Avoid:

- Repeated card grids
- Dashboard aesthetics
- Identical section templates
- Excessive containers
- Excessive rounded corners
- Decorative shadows

---

## 8. Typography System

Three typographic roles should exist.

### Display Serif

Purpose:

- Major statements
- Editorial headlines
- Manifesto moments
- Selected emphasis
- Italic expressions

Characteristics:

- High contrast
- Elegant
- Contemporary rather than classical
- Strong italic

Do not choose the final font before testing several families in-browser.

---

### Grotesk / Sans

Purpose:

- Navigation
- Body copy
- Interface
- Project information
- Services
- Functional content

Characteristics:

- Neutral
- Clean
- Highly legible
- International
- Not excessively geometric

---

### Mono

Purpose:

- Section identifiers
- Metadata
- Coordinates
- Project year
- Categories
- System labels
- ASCII graphics
- Technical information

Mono is part of the identity, not merely a code font.

Example:

`(01 — SELECTED WORK)`

---

## 9. Color System

Initial identity direction:

### Canvas

Warm off-white.

Reference role:

`--color-canvas`

Approximate starting point:

`#FBFBF9`

---

### Ink

Near-black.

`--color-ink: #1D1D1B`

Use near-black rather than pure `#000000` for most surfaces.

---

### Signal

Existing Urca warm orange.

`--color-signal: #D95A2F`

Signal is not a generic CTA color.

Use it intentionally for:

- Tiny markers
- Active states
- ASCII focal points
- Selected links
- Motion details
- System signals
- Special editorial emphasis

Avoid turning every button orange.

---

### Muted

A family of neutral grays should be derived later.

Do not create arbitrary gray values inside components.

---

## 10. Computational Identity

### Generative ASCII

ASCII is a signature visual primitive of Urca.

It replaces the generic gradient/blob language often used by contemporary digital studios.

ASCII should feel:

- Sculptural
- Abstract
- Minimal
- Generative
- Alive
- Quietly technical

ASCII should not feel:

- Hacker
- Terminal
- Matrix
- Retro-computer cosplay
- Cyberpunk
- Gamer UI

---

## 11. ASCII Character Language

Initial character ramp:

` · . : + * # % `

Alternative editorial character set may include selected Unicode glyphs:

`· : ∴ + × * ◦`

Unicode may be used when it produces a more refined composition, but the visual language should remain character-based.

Character sets must be centrally defined.

Do not hard-code arbitrary character ramps inside components.

---

## 12. ASCII Behaviors

Supported behaviors may include:

### Static
Frozen character composition.

### Breathe
Very slow density or shape deformation.

### Drift
Subtle procedural movement.

### Cursor
Limited response to pointer position.

### Dissolve
Characters progressively disperse or reconstruct.

### Image Translation
A source image is interpreted as character density.

### Morph
One abstract field transitions into another.

No effect should exist merely because it is technically impressive.

---

## 13. ASCII Usage Hierarchy

### Hero
High visibility.

A large abstract generative sculpture can act as the primary graphic.

### Project previews
Medium visibility.

Possible interaction:

`ASCII interpretation → actual project image`

### Section transitions
Low visibility.

Sparse fields, coordinates or procedural fragments.

### The Factory
Medium visibility.

ASCII may become diagrammatic and explain the studio process.

### Footer
Low visibility.

Small generative signature.

### 404 / Loading
Higher experimentation permitted.

---

## 14. Motion Principles

Motion should feel:

- Slow
- Precise
- Physical
- Restrained
- Responsive

Preferred motion:

- Text reveals
- Mask transitions
- Image reveals
- Density transitions
- ASCII morphing
- Subtle cursor response
- Smooth state changes

Avoid:

- Excessive parallax
- Constant floating
- Spring animation everywhere
- Page elements chasing the cursor
- Long intro sequences
- Scroll hijacking

Always respect:

`prefers-reduced-motion`

The website must remain fully understandable without animation.

---

## 15. Imagery

Client work should remain visually dominant.

Urca's own design language must frame projects, not overpower them.

Prefer:

- Art-directed project imagery
- Large crops
- Full-width media when justified
- Motion/video where valuable
- Project-specific color environments
- Product screens without unnecessary device frames

Avoid repeating identical mockups for every project.

---

## 16. Grid

Desktop foundation:

**12-column editorial grid**

The grid should provide structure without making every section visually symmetrical.

Use controlled grid violations where they improve composition.

Layouts should become progressively simpler on tablet and mobile rather than mechanically shrinking desktop compositions.

---

## 17. Interface Language

UI should be visually quiet.

Buttons, links, navigation and controls exist to support the content.

Preferred:

- Text links
- Thin rules
- Minimal borders
- Small directional symbols
- Typographic hover states

Avoid:

- Giant pills
- Gradient buttons
- Glassmorphism
- Heavy shadows
- Excessive radius
- Decorative cards

---

## 18. Logo Usage

The existing Urca identity remains the brand foundation.

Available lockups should be treated as master assets and must not be redrawn in CSS or recreated from text.

The digital design system should extend the existing identity rather than replace it.

---

## 19. Creative Reference

Kyne Jang is a reference for:

- Typographic confidence
- Whitespace
- Editorial composition
- Minimal interface
- Project-led storytelling
- Strong art direction

It is **not** a template.

Do not reproduce:

- Exact layouts
- Exact typography
- Exact animation
- Blob graphics
- Section compositions

Urca's differentiating device is its computational/ASCII layer.

---

## 20. Core Creative Rule

When choosing between:

**more UI**

and

**stronger composition**

prefer stronger composition.

When choosing between:

**more animation**

and

**better typography**

prefer better typography.

When choosing between:

**more ASCII**

and

**one memorable ASCII moment**

prefer the memorable moment.

---

## 21. Working Creative Definition

**Urca is an editorial design studio with the computational behavior of a software system.**
